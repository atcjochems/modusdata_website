Uncovering the Blind Spot: Screening for Iron Deficiency Beyond Hemoglobin

How a short questionnaire and lightweight machine learning can help decide who should get a ferritin test, and why routine blood counts alone can miss iron deficiency.

The Blind Spot in Routine Care

Iron deficiency is the most common micronutrient deficiency worldwide, and young women are at particularly high risk because of growth, menstrual blood loss and dietary habits.[1] Yet there are no official guidelines recommending screening for iron deficiency in healthy young women.

Routine blood work typically includes a complete blood count (CBC), which reports hemoglobin (Hb). Hemoglobin is useful for detecting anemia, but it is a late signal: iron stores can be depleted well before hemoglobin falls below the anemia threshold. The best single test for iron stores is serum ferritin, which is not part of a standard check-up.

The data in the source study illustrate the gap. Among 483 high school girls in Sweden, 183 (38%) had a ferritin below 15 µg/L, yet only 17.6% of those iron-deficient girls were anemic (Hb <120 g/L). Roughly four in five would have been missed by an anemia check alone.[1]

Why a Questionnaire Can Help

Testing ferritin in everyone is unlikely to be feasible in most healthcare systems. A short risk questionnaire offers a pragmatic alternative: it does not diagnose anything, but it helps prioritize who should be offered a confirmatory blood test.

That is the idea behind IRON-5, a five-item instrument developed by Vinge et al. (2026) from a cross-sectional study of high school girls aged 16–19 in Malmö and Lund, Sweden.[1] Each "risk" answer scores one point, for a total of 0 to 5:

Risk factor (1 point each) Odds ratio (95% CI)
Excludes red meat (pescatarian, vegetarian, vegan)3.70 (2.36–5.79)
No iron or iron-containing supplements2.61 (1.28–5.32)
Menstruation bothers you due to heavy bleeding2.30 (1.47–3.62)
No hormonal contraceptives2.13 (1.21–3.75)
Avoids activities during heavy menstrual days1.98 (1.30–3.00)

A score of 3 or more flags high risk. In the development sample this cut-off gave a sensitivity of 74%, specificity of 57%, positive predictive value of 51%, negative predictive value of 78% and an AUC of 0.72.[1] In other words, IRON-5 is a triage aid with good sensitivity and moderate specificity: some iron-deficient girls will be missed, and some who screen positive will turn out to be iron sufficient.

What the Evidence Does and Doesn't Show

  • Development, not external validation. The score was derived post hoc from one study population, and the authors state that further validation is required.
  • Narrow population. It was built on self-reported data from girls aged 16–19 and only applies to people who menstruate. Its performance in other age groups is unknown.
  • Definition of deficiency. The study used the WHO cut-off of ferritin <15 µg/L. Newer work suggests a higher physiological threshold of about 25 µg/L, which would classify more people as deficient.[1]
  • Benefit of treatment is still open. Evidence that iron treatment helps comes mainly from people seeking care for symptoms such as fatigue. Whether treating screen-detected, symptom-free iron deficiency is worthwhile remains to be shown.

Explore the Live App

To show the approach in practice, I built an interactive, dual-mode web application hosted on Streamlit Cloud. It combines a tabular machine learning predictor trained on public health data with an implementation of the published IRON-5 questionnaire and its scoring rule.

Inside the GitHub Repository

The pipeline, exploratory notebooks, trained models and application source code are open-sourced under the MIT license on GitHub.

  • Tabular ML classification: Logistic Regression and XGBoost binary classifiers trained on CBC-derived features to predict iron-risk categories.
  • IRON-5 scoring engine: A transparent, rule-based implementation of the five yes/no items (diet, menstrual bleeding burden, activity avoidance, iron supplements, hormonal contraception) and the ≥3 threshold.
  • Clean code structure: Modular utility functions, model serialization with joblib, and a Streamlit interface built for fast cold starts.
